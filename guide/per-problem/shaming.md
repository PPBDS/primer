### Shaming  *(standalone · Medium · one scenario: predictive)*

- **Type:** example (class exercise only)
- **Renderings:** chapter — · tutorial — · class exercise ✓ (`primer.exercises` repo, `shaming/`). The chapter ✓ and tutorial ✓ for this material belong to slot 10 and are causal; this exercise is a separate, predictive rendering (decided with David 2026-07-27).
- **"Imagine" (one, given to students verbatim):** "Imagine that you are the data scientist for a campaign for Governor of Texas. You want to predict which registered voters will vote in the upcoming primary election. Fortunately, the Texas voter file records, for every registered voter, their participation in past elections."
- **Preceptor's Question:** What proportion of registered voters in Texas will vote in the upcoming primary, and how does that proportion differ by a voter's record of participation in past elections?
- **Dataset:** `data/shaming.csv` — a **10,000-row random sample of the No Postcard control group** (191,243 voters) from `primer.data::shaming` (Gerber, Green, Larimer 2008 Michigan field experiment; 344,084 voters). Drawn with `set.seed(10)` + `slice_sample(n = 10000)` after `filter(treatment == "No Postcard")`; the `treatment` column is dropped. Control-only so that postcard-boosted turnout cannot leak into a no-postcard forecast, and 10K rows so `logistic_reg()` fits fast in class. Columns: `primary_06` (voted in the August 2006 Michigan primary, 0/1), `sex` (Female/Male), `age` (20–106, no NAs), and six past-participation indicators (`primary_00`, `general_00`, `primary_02`, `general_02`, `primary_04`, `general_04`). **By GGL design the data exclude never-voters** — everyone participated in at least one of the six prior elections — so `civ_engage` runs 1–6, never 0; a first-order representativeness discussion. Overall turnout 28.8%.
- **Underlying paper:** Gerber, Green, and Larimer (2008), "Social Pressure and Voter Turnout: Evidence from a Large-Scale Field Experiment," *APSR* 102(1) — in the folder as `social-pressure.pdf` (copied from `primer.data` `inst/papers/shaming.pdf`).
- **Outcome:** `voted` — cast a ballot in the 2006 primary (factor from `primary_06`)
- **Covariates:** `civ_engage` (count of the six prior elections the voter participated in, 1–6), `age`, `sex`
- **Causal / Predictive:** predictive. Deliberate: the causal rendering of this material lives in slot 10's chapter and tutorial, and the predictive frame also honors the guide's "probabilities are not outcomes" language (`concepts-and-drops.md` §12.1, `curriculum.md` commitment 8) — with no treatment there is no temptation to speak of "the causal effect on probabilities"; all probability statements are descriptions of groups of rows.
- **Data prep (in the setup chunk of every file — students receive it, don't write it):** read CSV → `mutate(civ_engage = primary_00 + primary_02 + primary_04 + general_00 + general_02 + general_04)` → `mutate(voted = as.factor(primary_06))` → `select(voted, sex, age, civ_engage)` → object `shaming`.
- **Candidate models (Courage):** `voted ~ age` (age matters: +0.018 log-odds/year, CI excludes zero) → `voted ~ age + sex` (`sexMale` +0.06, CI includes zero) → `+ civ_engage` (dominates: +0.49 per past election; age falls by more than half once engagement enters — older voters vote more largely because they carry longer participation histories).
- **Final model:** `logistic_reg() |> fit(voted ~ age + sex + civ_engage, data = shaming)` → `fit_shaming`. Equation: `logit(Pr(voted = 1)) = −3.22 + 0.0074·age + 0.070·male + 0.494·civ_engage`. `sex` stays despite its zero-spanning CI: the voter file always records it and the campaign wants forecasts for men and women; randomization is not the issue here — it simply costs nothing.
- **Headline numbers:** average predicted turnout by `civ_engage`: 7.7% (1) → 13.3% → 21.0% → 30.7% → 42.0% → 54.6% (6). `avg_comparisons` for one additional past election: **+10.2 pp [9.4, 11.1]**. Sex difference (Male − Female): +1.3 pp [−0.4, 3.1] — includes zero. The story: past participation separates likely from unlikely voters; demographics barely move the needle.
- **Preceptor Table:** Unit (Voter) | Outcome (Voted) | Covariate (Civic Engagement)
- **Population Table:** same plus Source and Unit/Time (Voter, Year). Data rows: Michigan control-group voters, 2006. Preceptor rows: Texas registered voters at the upcoming (2028) primary.
- **Assumption hooks:** validity — "voting in a primary" differs between Michigan's August 2006 primary and a Texas March primary (rules, timing, salience), and the six past-participation columns count *Michigan* contests while the Texas file counts Texas ones: same names, different things. Stability — turnout behavior has shifted since 2006 (early/mail voting, polarization-driven surges). Representativeness — the data are the control group of an experiment run on a *selected* Michigan sample that excluded never-voters entirely, while the campaign's contact list includes them; the model cannot speak to voters with `civ_engage = 0`. Unconfoundedness — not applicable: predictive model, no treatment.
